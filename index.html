    The Four Laws of Behavior Change

    Every habit runs a loop: cue → craving → response → reward. The system is built around that loop.

    1

    Make it obvious

    The cue must be visible. Use an implementation intention ("I will [behavior] at [time] in [location]") and habit stacking ("After [current habit], I will [new habit]").

    Break a habit: make it invisible – remove the cues from your environment.

    2

    Make it attractive

    Pair what you need to do with what you want to do (temptation bundling). Join a culture where your desired behavior is normal.

    Break a habit: make it unattractive – name its real cost.

    3

    Make it easy

    Reduce friction. Use the Two-Minute Rule – start with a version that takes under two minutes. Repetitions matter more than time spent.

    Break a habit: make it difficult – add friction, use a commitment device.

    4

    Make it satisfying

    What is rewarded is repeated. Track progress, don't break the chain, and never miss twice.

    Break a habit: make it unsatisfying – get an accountability partner.
